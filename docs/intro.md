Q1
Glymmy is an AI-powered health companion that connects your existing devices CGM, smartwatch, phone camera into one intelligent platform. Photograph your meal, and Glymmy identifies the food, estimates carbs, and predicts your glucose spike 30-60 minutes ahead using your personal trends. 

Upload a blood report, and it extracts every marker and explains abnormal values in plain language. It tracks overnight blood sugar patterns and alerts you not just when something is wrong, but why. All your health data glucose, vitals, nutrition, lab results lives in one unified profile built on open healthcare standards (FHIR), shareable with your doctor via a single QR code. 

Ideating on a foundation of proven tools Nightscout for CGM data, USDA for nutrition, and open health standards like FHIR Glymmy connects existing, validated components into the unified health platform that doesn't exist yet.


Q2
We want to participate because the problem Glymmy tackles is personal. Members of our team have seen family and friends struggle with diabetes management juggling multiple apps, deciphering confusing lab reports, and never getting a clear picture of how their daily choices affect their health. We've watched people make decisions in the dark about what to eat, when to exercise, and what their blood work actually means. That frustration is what drives this project.

The Harvard HSIL Hackathon is the right place for this idea because it sits at the intersection of AI and high-value health systems exactly where Glymmy lives. We're not just building a tech demo. We're trying to validate whether unifying fragmented health data into one AI-powered platform can genuinely change outcomes for chronic disease patients.

What we hope to get: honest feedback from healthcare professionals and fellow builders on whether our approach connecting existing proven tools rather than inventing from scratch — is the right architecture. We want to pressure-test our assumptions about what patients and doctors actually need, not what we think they need.

We also want to learn from teams tackling adjacent problems interoperability, clinical decision support, patient engagement because Glymmy touches all of these. 
The hackathon isn't just about winning. It's about sharpening an idea that we believe deserves to exist.
